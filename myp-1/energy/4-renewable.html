Q4.2 Brain teaser: Coal, oil and gas are called fossil fuels. They formed from plants and animals that lived millions of years ago. So where did the energy stored in them originally come from?
From the Sun! Ancient plants stored sunlight as chemical energy by photosynthesis; animals ate the plants. Buried for millions of years, their remains became coal, oil and gas. Burning them releases that ancient sunlight energy, plus carbon dioxide.

Q4.3 Record your team's design and score. Try a town with only solar farms: what happens at night? How can you fix it without fossil fuels?
With only solar, the lights go out at night (and the evening peak is the hardest time). Fixes: add batteries to store spare daytime energy, and mix sources such as wind (it can blow at night) and hydro (steady).
Q4.4 Why are fossil-fuel plants still useful, even though they pollute?
They give power whenever it is needed, day or night, whatever the weather, so they are reliable. But the fuel runs out, costs money, and burning it releases CO2 (climate change) and air pollution.
Criterion D

Lebanon's energy story

For years, Lebanon's national electricity company has supplied only a few hours of power a day in many areas. Most buildings rely on private diesel generators, which are noisy, expensive and polluting. Since 2022, many thousands of homes, schools and businesses have installed rooftop solar panels with batteries.

Q4.5 Sort each statement about solar panels and diesel generators in Lebanon.
Q4.6 Real world: Give one way rooftop solar helps Lebanese families and one way it helps the environment. Then give one problem it does not solve.
Families: electricity during power cuts and lower generator bills. Environment: less diesel burned, so less CO2, smoke and noise. Problem: panels are costly to buy, need space, and give no power at night without batteries.
Q4.7 Real world: The Qaraoun dam on the Litani river makes electricity. Write its energy chain.
Water held high behind the dam (gravitational potential) → falling water (kinetic) → spinning turbine (kinetic) → generator (electrical).
