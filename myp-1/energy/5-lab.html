Ramp racers

Lara's little brother races toy cars down a ramp in the school corridor. He says: "The higher I start the car, the farther it goes!" Is he right? Let's investigate like real scientists.

Q5.4 In your sheet, write a step-by-step method: how you will change the height, how you will measure the distance, and how many times you will repeat each height.
1. Mark a start line at the top of the ramp with tape. 2. Set the top of the ramp to 10 cm using books. 3. Place the car's front wheels on the line and let go without pushing. 4. When it stops, measure the distance from the bottom of the ramp to the back of the car with a tape measure. 5. Repeat 3 times for this height. 6. Repeat for 20, 30, 40 and 50 cm. 7. Work out the average distance for each height.

Conclusion and evaluation

Q5.6 Describe the trend in your graph. Use numbers from your data.
As the release height increases, the distance rolled increases. The points make a roughly straight line through the origin: doubling the height (e.g. 20 → 40 cm) roughly doubles the distance (about 125 → 250 cm on tiles).
Q5.7 Was your prediction supported? Explain using energy.
Yes. A higher start gives the car more gravitational potential energy, which becomes more kinetic energy at the bottom. More kinetic energy lets the car roll farther before friction turns it all into thermal energy and it stops.
Q5.8 Did you find an anomaly (a result that does not fit the pattern)? What might have caused it, and what should you do?
A trial far from the other two at the same height is an anomaly. Causes: the car was pushed, it hit something, or it was measured wrongly. Leave it out of the average and repeat that trial.
Q5.9 Evaluate the method: name two problems that could make the results less reliable, and suggest an improvement for each.
Problem: the car may be pushed slightly → improvement: use a ruler as a gate and lift it to release. Problem: hard to see exactly where the car stops / the car veers sideways → mark the stop point with tape, use guide rails, film it. Also: do more trials (5 per height) and test more heights.
Q5.10 Extension: Switch the floor to carpet and run the experiment again. Compare the two graphs and explain the difference.
On carpet the car rolls much less far from every height. Carpet causes more friction, so the car's kinetic energy is changed into thermal energy faster. The trend (higher → farther) is the same.
