Kinetic energy

The energy of movement. Faster → more kinetic energy. Heavier → more kinetic energy.

Gravitational potential energy

Energy stored by height. Higher → more. Heavier → more.

Q2.1 Where on the ramp is the skater's kinetic energy greatest? Where is the gravitational potential energy greatest?
Kinetic energy is greatest at the bottom (fastest). Gravitational potential energy is greatest at the top (highest), where the skater stops for a moment.
Q2.2 Set friction to none. What happens to the TOTAL bar? What does that tell you about energy?
The total stays the same: energy just swaps between gravitational potential and kinetic. Energy is not used up; it only changes form.
Q2.3 Now set friction to a lot. Why does the skater go less high each time? Where does the energy go?
Friction between the wheels, the ramp and the air changes some energy into thermal energy (and sound). This energy spreads out and cannot lift the skater, so we call it wasted. The total is still the same!
Q2.4 Brain teaser: Try the 30 kg and the 70 kg skater from the same height with no friction. Who is faster at the bottom? Who has more energy?
Surprise: they reach the bottom at the same speed! But the heavier skater has more energy (more gravitational potential at the top and more kinetic at the bottom), because more mass means more energy.

Q2.5 Copy the table for the basketball into your sheet. Describe the pattern in the bounce heights.
Each bounce is lower than the one before: the basketball reaches about 70% of the previous height each time. The heights get smaller and smaller until the ball stops.
Q2.6 Brain teaser: Why can a dropped ball never bounce higher than where it started? Where did the "missing" energy go?
A ball can't get more energy than it started with: energy can't be created. At each bounce some energy changes into sound (the "thud") and thermal energy in the ball and the floor, so less is left to lift it.
Q2.7 Real world: As the Jounieh–Harissa cable car climbs the mountain, which energy store of the cabin increases? Where does that energy come from?
Its gravitational potential energy increases as it gets higher. The energy comes from the electric motor that pulls the cable (electrical energy).
Q2.8 Real world: Why is the first hill of a roller coaster always the tallest?
At the top of the first hill the train has its most gravitational potential energy. That energy powers the rest of the ride. Friction wastes some energy all the way, so every later hill must be lower or the train would not get over it.
