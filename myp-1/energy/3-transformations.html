Station 3 · Energy transformations

Energy changes form

An energy transformation is when energy changes from one form into another. Energy is never made and never destroyed: the total amount always stays the same. It just changes form.

Q3.1 For each step of the machine, write the energy change and what made it happen (the "change-maker").
1 Spring: elastic → kinetic (the spring pushes: a force). 2 Ramp: gravitational potential → kinetic (gravity pulls the ball down). 3 Dominoes: kinetic → kinetic (each push knocks the next). 4 Bell: kinetic → sound (the hit makes the bell vibrate). 5 Lamp: chemical → electrical → light + thermal (closing the switch lets an electric current flow).
Key idea

What makes the change happen?

Energy only changes form when something happens. We call these the change-makers:

💪 A force
a push, a pull, gravity or friction
🔥 Heating
something hot warms something cooler
⚡ An electric current
flows when a circuit is closed
🧪 A chemical reaction
burning, digesting food, a battery working
☀️ Light shining
sunlight landing on a solar panel or a leaf
🔊 Vibration
a moving object makes the air shake

Q3.4 Which bulb wastes more energy? During a power cut, why do Lebanese families choose LEDs for their solar batteries?
The old filament bulb wastes much more: most of its energy becomes thermal energy (it gets very hot). An LED turns much more of the energy into light, so the same battery lights the house for much longer.
Q3.5 A kettle takes in 200 J of electrical energy. 150 J heats the water. How much energy is wasted?
200 J − 150 J = 50 J wasted (as thermal energy warming the air and the kettle, and a little sound). Energy in = energy out.
Q3.6 Brain teaser: If energy is never destroyed, why do we keep having to pay for electricity and fuel?
The energy is not destroyed, but it ends up as spread-out thermal energy (warm air, warm walls). Spread-out energy is very hard to collect and use again, so we need new useful energy.
Q3.7 Real world: Many buildings in Aramoun use a diesel generator during power cuts. Write the energy chain from the diesel to the light in your room, and name two forms of wasted energy you can notice near a generator.
Diesel (chemical) → engine moving (kinetic) → generator (electrical) → lamp (light). Wasted: sound (the loud noise) and thermal energy (the hot engine and exhaust).
