Performance task

GRASPS: an energy plan for our school

D Reflecting on the impacts of science · individual task, completed over 1–2 weeks
G · GoalDesign a plan that keeps the school's lights, fans and computers working during power cuts, with as little fuel and pollution as possible.
R · RoleYou are a member of the school's Green Energy Team, a young energy engineer.
A · AudienceThe school principal and the parents' committee, who will decide what to buy.
S · SituationThe national grid supplies only a few hours a day in Aramoun. The school runs a diesel generator that is loud, expensive and polluting. The school has a large flat roof and a limited budget.
P · ProductA poster or a 3-minute presentation (see the checklist below).
S · StandardsAssessed with Criterion D (rubric below). Use the scientific words from this unit.

Your product must include

  1. An energy transformation diagram for the diesel generator and for your new system, showing useful and wasted energy.
  2. Your chosen energy sources, saying which are renewable or non-renewable, with at least 2 advantages and 2 disadvantages for each.
  3. One way the school can waste less energy (for example LEDs, switching off, using daylight).
  4. How your plan affects people (health, noise, learning), money (costs, savings) and the environment (CO₂, air pollution).
  5. A sources list: website title, author or organization, date, link.

Helpful starting points

  • Station 4 game: test your mix of sources before you choose.
  • Station 3 Sankey diagrams: compare LEDs with old bulbs.
  • Ask the school administration how many hours the generator runs each week.
  • Interview a family member about how your home copes with power cuts.

Task-specific rubric · Criterion D (MYP Year 1)

LevelWhat your work shows
0The work does not reach any of the descriptors below.
1–2You state how energy science can help the school's power problem; you state one effect of your plan on people, money or the environment; you use some scientific words, with limited success; you list sources with limited success.
3–4You outline how your chosen energy sources solve the problem; you outline effects on people, money or the environment; you sometimes use scientific words (kinetic, potential, transformation, renewable…) correctly; you sometimes document sources correctly.
5–6You summarize how your plan uses energy science to solve the problem, with a clear transformation diagram; you describe effects on people, money and the environment; you usually use scientific language clearly and precisely; you usually document sources correctly.
7–8You summarize in detail how your plan uses energy science, comparing renewable and non-renewable options; you describe and summarize the effects on people, money and the environment, including at least one drawback of your own plan; you consistently use scientific language clearly and precisely; you document all sources completely.
