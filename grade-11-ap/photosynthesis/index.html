Photosynthesis: Following the Energy

A plant makes its own food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. It does this in two linked stages inside the chloroplast: the light reaction and the dark reaction.

The Bekaa greenhouse. In January, Rami's tomatoes in his greenhouse near Zahleh grow slowly. The days are short and cloudy. He wonders whether to switch on lamps over his plants. By the end of today you will advise him.

Part 1

The light reaction

The light-dependent reaction captures the energy of sunlight and stores it in two energy carriers: ATP and NADPH.

1.1 Location: thylakoids and grana

  • Chloroplast. The organelle where photosynthesis happens. Inside are many green stacks.
  • Granum. A stack of flat discs, like a stack of coins. Many stacks = grana.
  • Thylakoid. One flat disc. Its membrane holds chlorophyll. The light reaction happens here.
Q1. Label the granum and a thylakoid on the diagram on your sheet.
The whole stack is the granum; each single flat disc in it is a thylakoid.
Q2. The light reaction takes place in the ________ membranes, which are stacked into ________.
thylakoid membranes, stacked into grana.
Q3. Brain teaser: Why do you think a chloroplast has its thylakoids stacked into many grana instead of having just one big thylakoid?
Stacking packs a huge amount of thylakoid membrane into a tiny space. More membrane means more chlorophyll, so the chloroplast can catch more sunlight. (Like a library: many shelves hold more books than one wall.)

1.2 Steps of the light reaction: watch it happen

  1. 1. Chlorophyll in the thylakoid membrane absorbs sunlight.
  2. 2. The light energy is used to split water.
  3. 3. Oxygen from the water is released into the air.
  4. 4. ADP is charged up to make ATP.
  5. 5. NADP⁺ is loaded up to make NADPH.
  6. 6. ATP and NADPH carry the energy to the stroma, for the dark reaction.

    Q9. Describe what happens to the rate as light intensity increases.
    At first the rate rises quickly as light increases. Above about 60% it rises only a little and then levels off (a plateau).
    Q10. Brain teaser: Between 80% and 100% light, the plant gets more light but hardly makes more oxygen. Suggest why.
    Light is no longer the limiting factor. Something else is now in short supply, for example carbon dioxide for the dark reaction, or the temperature, so extra light cannot speed things up.
    Q11. Real world: Use the graph to explain why Rami's tomatoes grow slowly on short, cloudy January days.
    Low light means a slow light reaction, so less ATP and NADPH are made. With less energy, the dark reaction makes less glucose, so the plants grow slowly.
    Part 2

    The dark reaction

    The light-independent reaction (Calvin cycle) uses the energy in ATP and NADPH to build glucose from carbon dioxide.

    2.1 Location: the stroma

    The stroma is the thick fluid that fills the chloroplast and surrounds the grana. The dark reaction takes place here.

    Q12. The dark reaction takes place in the ________ of the chloroplast.
    stroma
    Q13. Why is the stroma a good place for the dark reaction, right next to the thylakoids?
    The ATP and NADPH made in the thylakoids are released straight into the stroma, so the dark reaction can use them immediately.

    2.2 Steps of the dark reaction: the sugar factory

    1. 1. CO₂ from the air enters the stroma.
    2. 2. CO₂ is fixed (joined) into the Calvin cycle.
    3. 3. ATP and NADPH supply the energy to build sugar.
    4. 4. Glucose is made. The plant uses it or stores it as starch.

      Q17. Brain teaser: The dark reaction does not use light directly. So does it keep making glucose all night long? Explain.
      No. It needs the ATP and NADPH made by the light reaction. In the dark, these run out within seconds, so glucose production stops. "Dark reaction" means it does not need light directly, not that it works in the dark.

      Q18. Plot the data on your sheet. What happens to glucose production after the light goes off?
      It stays steady while the light is on, then falls quickly after minute 5 and reaches zero by minute 8.
      Q19. Explain why glucose production stops, even though carbon dioxide is still available.
      With no light, the light reaction stops, so no new ATP and NADPH are made. The last ATP and NADPH are used up, and without this energy CO₂ cannot be turned into glucose.
      Q20. Brain teaser: Predict what happens if a plant gets more ATP and NADPH than usual. Will glucose production rise forever? Test it with the slider.
      More energy means more glucose, but only up to a point. Then the factory runs short of another input, carbon dioxide, and the rate levels off.
      Part 3

      Connect the two reactions

      Q21. Which two molecules carry energy from the light reaction to the dark reaction?
      ATP and NADPH.
      Q22. Advise Rami (Claim – Evidence – Reasoning). Should Rami switch on lamps over his tomatoes on cloudy January days? Use the light-intensity graph as evidence, and explain using both reactions.
      Claim: Yes, the lamps should help on cloudy days.
      Evidence: In 1.5, the rate rises steeply at low light (from 15 to 23 bubbles per minute between 20% and 40%).
      Reasoning: More light speeds up the light reaction, making more ATP and NADPH. The dark reaction can then turn more CO₂ into glucose, so the plants grow faster. On very bright days the extra light would add little, because the rate levels off.
