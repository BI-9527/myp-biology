Q1.2 Name two things that changed in your body after jogging. Why do you think they changed?
You breathe faster and your heart beats faster. Working muscles need more oxygen to release more energy from glucose, and they must get rid of more carbon dioxide. This energy-releasing process is cellular respiration.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP)

Q1.4 Brain teaser: Compare this equation with the photosynthesis equation you learned. What do you notice?
It is the reverse. Photosynthesis uses CO2 + H2O + light energy to make glucose + O2. Respiration uses glucose + O2 to release the energy again, giving back CO2 + H2O. Revisit photosynthesis.

Q1.5 Label the mitochondrion in your booklet: outer membrane, inner membrane, cristae, matrix, intermembrane space.
Turn on Labels and cut the model open. The matrix is the fluid inside the inner membrane; the intermembrane space is the narrow gap between the two membranes; the cristae are the folds of the inner membrane.
Q1.6 Brain teaser: The inner membrane is folded into many cristae. Why might this help the cell?
Folds give a much larger surface area in a small space. The machinery that makes most of the ATP sits on the inner membrane, so more membrane means more ATP. (Just like stacking thylakoids into grana in the chloroplast.)
Q1.7 Real world: Heart muscle cells and the leg muscles of trained runners are packed with mitochondria. Fat-storage cells have very few. Explain why.
Heart and leg muscles work all the time and need huge amounts of ATP, so they need many mitochondria. Endurance training like Lara's actually increases the number of mitochondria in her muscles. Fat cells mainly store energy and use little ATP.
3D lab

ATP: the cell's rechargeable battery

Drag to turn the molecule
Energy used: 0 times
  • ATP = adenosine triphosphate: adenine + ribose sugar + 3 phosphate groups.
  • Breaking off the last phosphate releases energy for muscle contraction, active transport, building molecules…
  • What is left is ADP (adenosine diphosphate, 2 phosphates) + a free phosphate.
  • Cellular respiration uses the energy in glucose to recharge ADP back into ATP.
Q1.8 What is the difference between ATP and ADP?
ATP has three phosphate groups and stores more energy. ADP has two. Removing one phosphate from ATP releases energy; adding it back (using energy from respiration) recharges it.
Q1.9 Brain teaser: Your body holds only about 250 g of ATP at any moment, yet every day you use roughly your own body mass in ATP. How is that possible?
ATP is recycled. Each molecule is used (ATP → ADP) and recharged (ADP → ATP) over and over, hundreds of times a day. Respiration never stops recharging it, which is why you need a constant supply of glucose and oxygen.
