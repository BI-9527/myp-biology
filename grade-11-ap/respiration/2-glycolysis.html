Glycolysis: splitting sugar

Glyco = sugar, lysis = splitting. Lara's breakfast has been digested into glucose, carried by her blood into a leg-muscle cell. Stage 1 begins in the cytoplasm, before glucose ever reaches a mitochondrion.

Q2.1 Where in the cell does glycolysis happen? Watch the first step of the animation, then write it in your booklet.
In the cytoplasm, the fluid outside the mitochondria. Glycolysis does not need oxygen.

  1. 1. Glucose (6 carbons) enters the cytoplasm.
  2. 2. 2 ATP are spent to activate the glucose (like a match lighting a fire).
  3. 3. The glucose is split into two 3-carbon halves.
  4. 4. Energy is captured: 4 ATP and 2 NADH are made. Each half becomes pyruvate.
  5. 5. If oxygen is present, the 2 pyruvates move into the mitochondrion.

    cytoplasm⟶  2+ 2(net)+ 2
    Q2.4 Brain teaser: Glycolysis is like opening a shop: you spend money before you earn any. It spends 2 ATP and makes 4 ATP. What is the profit (net gain)?
    4 made − 2 spent = 2 ATP net gain per glucose.
    Q2.5 Carbon count: glucose has 6 carbons. How many carbons does each pyruvate have? Were any carbons lost?
    Each pyruvate has 3 carbons. 2 × 3 = 6, so no carbon is lost in glycolysis: no CO2 is released yet. That happens in Mission 3.

    Q2.7 Brain teaser: Glycolysis happens in almost every living thing on Earth, from bacteria to humans, and it needs no oxygen. What does this suggest about how old it is?
    It is probably one of the oldest energy pathways. Early Earth had almost no oxygen, so the first cells needed a way to get ATP without it. All their descendants kept glycolysis.
    Q2.8 Real world: Why can't Lara run a marathon using glycolysis alone?
    Glycolysis gives only 2 ATP per glucose. Aerobic respiration gives about 36. Using glycolysis alone, she would need about 18 times more glucose for the same energy. Most of her ATP must come from the mitochondria.
