The Krebs cycle: the CO2 factory

With oxygen available, Lara's two pyruvates enter a mitochondrion. Inside the matrix their carbon atoms are stripped off one by one and leave as carbon dioxide, the same CO2 she breathes out with every step.

Q3.1 In which part of the mitochondrion does the Krebs cycle happen?
In the matrix, the fluid inside the inner membrane.

Press “Enter the matrix”
  1. Link step. Each pyruvate (3 C) loses one carbon as CO₂ and makes 1 NADH. The 2-carbon piece left is acetyl-CoA.
  2. Join. Acetyl (2 C) joins a 4-carbon molecule waiting in the cycle → a 6-carbon molecule (citric acid).
  3. Lose carbon. Around the cycle, 2 carbons leave as 2 CO₂. Energy is loaded onto 3 NADH and 1 FADH₂, and 1 ATP is made.
  4. Back to the start. The 4-carbon molecule is rebuilt, ready for the next acetyl. That is why it is a cycle.
Q3.2 What happens to a pyruvate as it enters the mitochondrion (the link step)?
It loses one carbon as CO2, makes 1 NADH, and the remaining 2-carbon piece (acetyl-CoA) enters the Krebs cycle.
Q3.3 Brain teaser: How many times must the cycle turn to deal with ONE glucose? Explain.
Twice. One glucose gives 2 pyruvates (Mission 2) and each pyruvate feeds one turn.
Q3.4 Press Run one whole glucose and read the counters. Complete the table in your booklet: per glucose, the link step + Krebs cycle make … CO₂, … NADH, … FADH₂, … ATP.
6 CO₂ (all 6 carbons of glucose are now gone!) · 8 NADH · 2 FADH₂ · 2 ATP.

Q3.6 Brain teaser: The Krebs cycle never uses oxygen itself, yet it stops within seconds if oxygen runs out. Suggest why. (Hint: what happens if all the NAD⁺ is turned into NADH and never emptied?)
The cycle needs a steady supply of empty carriers (NAD⁺ and FAD). Only the electron transport chain empties NADH and FADH2, and the chain needs oxygen at its end (Mission 4). No oxygen → carriers stay full → the cycle jams.

The limewater breath test

Cloudiness: 0% · clear

Limewater is clear, but turns milky when carbon dioxide bubbles through it. Invite students to the board: one pumps room air, one breathes out. Count the presses each needs to turn the limewater milky.

Q3.7 Predict, then test: which turns limewater milky faster, the air you breathe in or the air you breathe out? Explain using the Krebs cycle.
Breathed-out air: it contains about 100 times more CO2 (about 4% instead of about 0.04%). That extra CO2 was made in your mitochondria by the link step and the Krebs cycle.
Q3.8 Real world: When a person loses 10 kg of body fat, where does most of that mass actually go?
Mostly out through the lungs as CO2 (about 8 kg of the 10 kg); the rest leaves as water. The carbon atoms of fat are released as CO2 in the link step and Krebs cycle. (Meerman & Brown, BMJ, 2014.)
