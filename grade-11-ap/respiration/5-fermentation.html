Q5.2 Explain why breathing rate rises during exercise.
Working muscles need much more ATP, so they need more oxygen for aerobic respiration (the electron transport chain) and must get rid of more CO2. Breathing stays fast after exercise to repay the oxygen debt and break down lactic acid.

Q5.4 Brain teaser: Turning pyruvate into lactic acid makes no extra ATP. So why do muscle cells bother?
It turns NADH back into NAD⁺. Glycolysis needs NAD⁺ to keep running. Without fermentation the NAD⁺ would run out and even the 2 ATP from glycolysis would stop.
Q5.5 Real world: Lara's legs burn in the final sprint, and she keeps breathing hard for minutes after crossing the line. Explain both.
Short of oxygen, her muscles made ATP by lactic acid fermentation; lactic acid built up and her muscles became acidic and tired (the burn). Afterwards she breathes hard to repay the oxygen debt: oxygen is needed to clear the lactic acid and restart full aerobic respiration.

Q5.7 Record each team's temperature and balloon size. Which temperature worked best? Why did the hottest flasks fail?
Around 35–40 °C is best. In the cold the yeast's enzymes work slowly. Above about 55 °C the enzymes are denatured and the yeast dies, so no CO2 is made. With no sugar, nothing happens at any temperature: no glucose, no fermentation.
Q5.8 Real world: Lebanese bakers leave man'oushe dough in a warm place before baking, and labneh is made by adding bacteria to milk. Use fermentation to explain each.
Dough: yeast ferments the sugars and makes CO2 bubbles that make the dough rise; the ethanol evaporates in the hot oven, and warmth speeds up the yeast's enzymes. Labneh/yogurt: bacteria carry out lactic acid fermentation; the lactic acid makes the milk sour and thick.

Q5.10 How many times more ATP does aerobic respiration release from one glucose than fermentation? Why is that?
About 36 ÷ 2 = 18 times more. Fermentation only uses glycolysis; the energy left in lactic acid or ethanol is never released. Aerobic respiration finishes the job in the Krebs cycle and electron transport chain.
