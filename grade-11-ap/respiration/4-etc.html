The ATP turbine

The NADH and FADH2 from Missions 2 and 3 are full of energy. Now they cash it in. On the folded inner membrane, a chain of proteins passes electrons along, pumps H+, and spins a tiny turbine, ATP synthase, that makes most of Lara's ATP.

Q4.1 Where exactly is the electron transport chain?
In the inner mitochondrial membrane, especially the cristae. That is why the folds matter: more membrane = more chains = more ATP.

Live: ATP made per 10 seconds

  1. 1. NADH and FADH₂ drop off high-energy electrons (they become NAD⁺ and FAD again).
  2. 2. The electrons are passed from protein to protein along the inner membrane.
  3. 3. Their energy is used to pump H⁺ into the intermembrane space.
  4. 4. H⁺ flow back through ATP synthase, which spins and turns ADP into ATP.
  5. 5. At the end, oxygen catches the electrons and joins with H⁺ to form water.
Q4.2 Put the steps of the electron transport chain in order.
    Q4.3 Brain teaser: The Qaraoun dam on the Litani river holds water high behind a wall; when the water rushes down through turbines, they spin and make electricity. In the mitochondrion, what plays the part of the dam, the water and the turbine?
    Dam = the inner membrane · water piled up behind it = H⁺ ions in the intermembrane space · turbine = ATP synthase, which spins as H⁺ flow through and makes ATP.
    Q4.4 What is the job of oxygen in the chain? What does it become?
    Oxygen is the final electron acceptor: it catches the used electrons at the end of the chain and joins with H⁺ to form water. Without it the electrons have nowhere to go.

    Effect of oxygen

    Q4.6 Use the Oxygen supply slider: let the graph run at 100%, then drop to 50%, then 0%. Describe and explain what happens to ATP production.
    Less oxygen → fewer electrons can leave the chain → electrons pass more slowly → fewer H⁺ pumped → ATP synthase slows → less ATP. At 0% the chain jams and ATP production from the ETC stops, once the H⁺ already pumped have flowed back.
    Q4.7 Real world: Cyanide blocks the last protein of the chain. Press ☠ Add cyanide. Why is cyanide deadly within minutes, even though the blood is still full of oxygen?
    Electrons can no longer be handed to oxygen, so the whole chain stops and almost no ATP is made. Cells that need huge amounts of ATP, especially in the heart and brain, fail within minutes.
    Q4.8 Real world: At the top of Mount Everest each breath holds only about one third of the oxygen at sea level. Why do climbers feel weak and slow up there?
    Less oxygen reaches the mitochondria, so the electron transport chain runs slower and muscles make less ATP. Climbers spend weeks acclimatising, and most use bottled oxygen.
    Q4.9 Brain teaser: the ATP bill. Add up the ATP for one glucose: glycolysis … + Krebs cycle … + electron transport chain … = …
    2 + 2 + about 32 = about 36 ATP per glucose. Nearly 90% comes from the electron transport chain, which needs oxygen.
