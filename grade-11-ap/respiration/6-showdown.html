Q6.1 Use the 3D model: what does the chloroplast pass to the mitochondrion, and what comes back?
Chloroplast → mitochondrion: glucose and oxygen. Mitochondrion → chloroplast: carbon dioxide and water. The products of one process are the inputs of the other.
Q6.2 Brain teaser: Switch the sunlight off. Which organelle stops first? Can the other keep going forever? Explain.
The chloroplast stops right away: no light, no photosynthesis. The mitochondrion keeps going while stored glucose and oxygen last, but it cannot go on forever: in the end, the energy in all food comes from the Sun.

Q6.5 Advise Lara's coach (Claim – Evidence – Reasoning). Next year Lara wants to beat her time. In the last kilometre she slows down and her legs burn. Her coach wants to know why, and whether months of endurance training will help. Use at least three things you learned in Missions 1–5.
Claim: Lara slows because her muscles run short of oxygen and switch to lactic acid fermentation; endurance training will help.
Evidence: Aerobic respiration releases about 36 ATP per glucose but fermentation only 2 (Mission 5). The ETC stops without oxygen (Mission 4). Breathing rate rises with exercise (Mission 5 data). Trained muscles contain more mitochondria (Mission 1).
Reasoning: In the final sprint her heart and lungs cannot supply enough oxygen, so the electron transport chain slows. Her muscles rely on glycolysis + lactic acid fermentation: little ATP, and lactic acid builds up and causes the burn. Endurance training gives her muscles more mitochondria and a better oxygen supply, so more ATP is made aerobically and fermentation starts later.
